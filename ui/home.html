Getting a key

  1. Sign in at themoviedb.org (or make a free account).
  2. Go to Settings → API. If you don't have a key yet, request a developer key there.
  3. Copy the API Read Access Token, the long one, and paste it here from your phone, or copy it in the app and come back.

More free services

Where-to-watch data from TMDB often misses titles on Tubi, Pluto TV, The Roku Channel and other free services. A free Watchmode key fills those gaps, and adds direct links to titles where it has them.

Trakt

Trakt keeps track of what you watch, anywhere, and suggests what to watch next. Connect it to see your Trakt watchlist and Trakt's picks for you on Home, and to add titles to your watchlist from their pages.

Leave this off if your Jellyfin server already sends what you watch to Trakt (the Jellyfin Trakt plugin), or everything gets counted twice.

How the app fills the screen.

On by default. Off puts the app in a normal window (handy on a desktop). Starting it with --windowed (as npm run dev does) always opens a window.

Small, quick movements as things appear and change. Off: everything moves straight to where it's going (also when your system is set to reduce motion).

    Live TV

    Channels come from IPTV channel lists (like FastChannels) and from Live TV on your Jellyfin servers. A channel that's in several places shows once, and plays from the first place on this list that has it (the next one if that doesn't work).

    When a channel comes in several versions (HD and SD, or from different places), the sharpest one plays first, then the rest in the order above. Sharpness comes from the channel's name ("HD", "4K"…) and is measured whenever a copy plays.

    Channels from channel lists are checked in the background every so often (and whenever one fails to play). Ones that don't open are hidden from the guide.

    iptv-org

    A free, community-run collection of public live streams from around the world. Add channels by country, language or category.

    Possible duplicates

    These have the same name, but their guides don't confirm they're the same channel (two cities' stations of one network look like this). Check watches both for about 40 seconds and compares the pictures; Check all does that for each pair and sorts them out for you, leaving only the ones it can't tell.

    Jellyfin

    Add your Jellyfin servers. Anything you have there gets its own button on the title's page.

    Plex

    Sign in to Plex to use your Plex servers, and ones shared with you, the same way: Jump back in, Up next, Library, and a button on each title's page when it's there.

    Plex titles play in mpv (with resume points, watched marks, and intro and credits skipping when your server has marked them). Without mpv, they open in Plex's own web player.

    Music

    Add your Navidrome server (or another Subsonic server). Albums you have there get a button on the album's page and their own shelf on the artist's page, and play in mpv.

    Your password isn't saved: only a scrambled form of it that your server can check. It's never put in a web address.

    Which folders count

    Pick the folders on your server that the Music tab looks in. With none picked, it looks in all of them. (Your server lists its folders as separate libraries.)

    Radio

    Stations you add play alongside your favourites. Paste a stream's address, or an M3U or PLS playlist.

    Stations come from Radio Browser, a free list kept by its listeners, and SomaFM. They play in mpv: ◀ and ▶ (previous and next) change station.

    Your music services

    Each one you switch on gets a button on every album's page.

    Playlists made for you, and artists like the ones you look at.

    ListenBrainz makes playlists for you from what you listen to (Navidrome can send it your plays). Give your ListenBrainz name and they appear on the Music tab as Made for you.

    Related artists come from ListenBrainz with no setup. A free Last.fm key gives Last.fm's instead, which often know more artists.

    Jellyfin Desktop

    Installed something just now? Check again. Apps are found as Flatpaks, regular installs (from your distro, the AUR and so on, through their app-launcher entries), and AppImages in your Applications, Downloads or ~/.local/bin folder.     Which screen mpv opens on.

    Automatic is the screen the app is on. If mpv still opens on another one (some desktops, Wayland ones especially, decide for themselves), pick the screen here. It's remembered.

    Controller

    Typing with the controller.

    L2 or R2 on its own works like holding Shift: while you hold it you type capitals and the second character on each key (! instead of 1), and letting go goes back. Pressing L2 and R2 together turns caps lock on, and pressing both again turns it off; with caps lock on, holding L2 or R2 types small letters. Start is Done. Each key that has a button of its own shows it.

    Pick an action, then press the button you want for it. The D-pad always moves around.

    Developer

    The app keeps a log of what it does: handy when something doesn't work. Passwords, keys and sign-ins are never written to it.

    Adds much more detail: every request to your servers, what a live channel offers, and mpv's own log of what it played. Turn it on, try the thing that isn't working, then get the log.

    Send the log to your phone, or start it afresh.
